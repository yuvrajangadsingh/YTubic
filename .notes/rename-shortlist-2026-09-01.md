# PART 1 — RANKING

## Before the list, one thing that reshuffles the weights

This app almost certainly cannot ship on the Mac App Store. It is an unofficial client for a streaming service and it is GPL-3.0. That is my inference, not something I verified, but if it holds, then App Store collisions matter mostly as evidence of live commercial use for trademark purposes, not as blocked shelf space. The channels that actually decide this are:

1. **Homebrew cask token.** This is the one that killed `alcove` outright, and it is the string a user types. All five names below have both the formula and cask token free.
2. **The repo path** under his own namespace, so contested org handles are noise.
3. **The domain**, and `.app` or `.fm` is enough.
4. **Plain web search**, because a free app spreads by someone posting a link and someone else googling the name.

Judged that way, some "crowded" verdicts get lighter and a couple get heavier.

## 1. radif — the recommendation

**Said:** ra-DEEF. Two syllables, stress on the second. An English speaker who guesses "RAD-if" is close enough and nothing bad happens.

**Means:** In a ghazal, the word or phrase that repeats at the end of every couplet. The thing the whole poem is built to return to. In Persian classical music it is also the name of the entire canonical repertoire.

**The Arabic worry, resolved.** The clearing pass flagged, honestly and as unverified, that the root ردف might carry a rump sense. I checked. English Wiktionary lists only the poetry sense (plus Indonesian "rime" and Uzbek). Rekhta's Urdu dictionary gives five senses for ردیف: the ghazal refrain; alphabetical arrangement of ghazals; a star near Aquila that rises as its opposite star sets; a rearguard force; and a person riding pillion behind a rider. Nothing anatomical, nothing vulgar. The "one riding behind" etymology is real and it is sense five, benign. I did not consult an Arabic monolingual dictionary for ردف as a separate word and I am not a native speaker, so if you want belt and braces, ask one Urdu-reading friend once. But the word you would actually ship is clean.

That star sense is a bonus nobody spotted. A star that rises as another sets is a good thing to own for a night-time fullscreen player, and it gives the icon somewhere to go.

**Why it fits:** You write ghazals, so this is vocabulary you already own rather than a word you went shopping for. It sits on the exact seam this product sits on, poetry and music at once. A library is the set of things you return to, which is literally what a radif is. And it says nothing about audio, so every exhausted root on your banned list is dodged at once. Five letters, lowercase, no diacritics, no spelling fork.

**Availability:** The strongest position on the list after chapbook, and the only Indian-language candidate that came back clear. npm free. Homebrew formula and cask both free. No app on either store under the name. No software trademark surfaced. `radif.app`, `radif.fm` and `radif.to` all available. The only same-name product anywhere is an Arabic synonyms dictionary. Web page one is Wikipedia on the poetic term, Rekhta, and the Persian music repertoire, which is the ideal shape: real, educational, no commercial holder to displace.

**Weakest point:** The name carries zero information to a stranger. Someone reads "radif" on Reddit and learns nothing about what it is. `shuck` at least had an English verb behind it that clicked once you were told. This one only clicks for people who read ghazals. That is a real cost for a free app, and it is the subject of Part 3.

## 2. gamak

**Said:** GUH-muk. Two syllables, hard g, hard k.

**Means:** The oscillation placed on a single note in Indian classical music, the ornament that gives one note weight. From Sanskrit *gam*, to move. Literally the movement inside a note.

**Why it fits:** This is the best product-argument fit of anything on the list. The whole reason this app exists is that you care what a note sounds like at 271k against 130k. Gamak is the tradition's own word for the richness inside a single note. It also ends hard, which puts it in the same mouth-shape as shuck and vemb. One settled Hindustani spelling, no fork, no diacritics, no English misread.

**Availability, and what the crowding costs:** npm free, Homebrew both free, no media app, no Mac app, nothing in software of any size. `gamak.app`, `.fm` and `.to` all available. The two things against it are both outside the category. `gamak.com` belongs to a Turkish electric motor manufacturer that has been operating since 1960 and holds a class 7 mark, machines, not software. And гамак is the ordinary Russian word for hammock, which is why the only two exact App Store hits are Russian aerial-yoga apps. Cost in practice: you never get the .com, and Russian-language search returns hammocks. Neither touches a Mac music player, neither is your market, and no class 9 filing surfaced.

**Weakest point:** You share the string with a 65-year-old manufacturer, so if this ever grew commercial legs there is a real company on the other side of the word. Also the meaning is one layer more technical than radif. Explaining "the shake on a note" takes a beat longer than explaining "the refrain in a ghazal", and fewer people have the hook to hang it on.

## 3. sneck

**Said:** rhymes with deck.

**Means:** The iron latch on a door. Northern English and Scots, attested from the early 1300s, effectively dead in speech.

**Why it fits:** Structurally this is the only one of the top three that makes the `shuck` move. A plain physical object used slant. A latch is not the room and it is not the music, it is the small piece of hardware you lift to get in, which is the honest description of a client app. Five letters, lowercase, hard ending, sits beside shuck and greens without effort.

**Availability:** The cleanest register result here. Three trademarks total, all in leather goods and clothing (s.Oliver, GB and EUIPO class 18/25) plus one dead US class 20. Nothing in class 9, 38, 41 or 42 in any of the three registers. npm free, Homebrew both free, no app on either store, `sneck.app` available and verified with two methods. Web page one is nothing but dictionaries, which is the best possible starting position: a real word with an empty commercial first page and room to become the first result.

**Weakest point, and it is two things, not one.** The clearing pass caught `sneak`, one letter away, same length, same initial cluster, negative connotation, and that is genuinely bad for an unofficial client people already half-suspect. What it also surfaced without drawing the conclusion: every exact-name repo on GitHub is a Snake clone, because "sneck" already reads to the internet as snake plus neck, and `snek` is a live meme spelling. So the word has two bad glances waiting, sneak and snake, and neither is about music. Also it is inert. It will never mean more than what you tell people it means.

## 4. chapbook

**Said:** CHAP-book.

**Means:** The cheap stitched pamphlet peddlers sold on the street, mostly ballads and poems. The printed words that travelled with the song.

**Why it fits:** It is the oldest version of what this app does, put the words in front of the person hearing them, and this app is the one that shows the lyrics and the artwork instead of hiding them behind a now-playing bar. It comes out of your own furniture, poetry and ninety books, so you can defend it without effort. The clearing found a bonus: chhaap (छाप / چھاپ) in Hindi, Urdu and Punjabi means imprint, stamp, seal, so an Urdu reader gets a second correct reading for free.

**Availability:** The best of the whole list. npm, PyPI, crates and Homebrew all free. No exact-name app on Mac or iOS. `chapbook.fm`, `.to` and `.sh` all available. Only collision is klembot/chapbook, a Twine story format at 98 stars, different category, no commercial weight.

**Weakest point:** Eight letters and a compound. Your register is five to six, single-token. This is the one you would feel every day in a Dock label next to shuck and vemb, and it points at print rather than sound so the story has to do all the work. Nothing is in the way, it just is not your shape.

## 5. tarz

**Said:** TARZ, one syllable.

**Means:** Manner, style, and in Urdu poetry specifically the tune a ghazal is set to. "Is tarz par" means to that melody. The mould a poem gets poured into.

**Why it fits:** Four letters, one syllable, the exact register as vemb, and the meaning is the moment words become a tune, which is your seam precisely. Registries are close to empty: npm free, Homebrew nothing, no Mac app, no media app anywhere, no US software mark. `tarz.fm` and `tarz.to` free.

**What the crowding costs, and it is perception not registries:** Search the four letters on GitHub and you get Tarzan, every time. The top ten is nothing but Tarzan repos. Second, inside developer context the string reads as an archive format. `.tar.Z` is real and Homebrew's nearest match was `tarlz`. A Dock icon labelled `tarz` next to a terminal reads as a compression utility. Third, the Turkish register was never searched and tarz is an everyday Turkish word in fashion, so TR marks are likely.

**Weakest point:** No English anchor at all. Page one is Turkish. The name is beautiful in Urdu and illegible everywhere else, and unlike radif there is no compensating cleanliness because the Tarzan pull is permanent.

# PART 2 — ICON DIRECTION

Three shared rules first.

**The container.** Keep the macOS squircle. The current icon system builds light, dark, tinted and clear variants from a layered source, so author it as a background layer plus a foreground layer and check that the foreground still reads when the system flattens the ground. The constraint you were given is no red rounded rectangle with a play triangle. A non-red squircle with a non-play mark is the platform convention, not a copy of anything.

**The menu bar.** Ship a separate hand-drawn 16pt and 32pt template image. Do not downscale the 512. A template image is single-colour with alpha, tinted by the system and inverted on highlight, so the ground disappears and only the silhouette survives. Each concept below says what its silhouette reduces to.

**No red anywhere**, including as an accent, including in the 512 texture pass. Not because red is owned, but because red plus a media app plus a rounded rect is the exact lockup you are running from.

---

## radif — the aligned terminal

**Core shape.** Two horizontal ink strokes, stacked, of clearly different lengths, both terminating on a shared right-hand vertical. At that vertical, each stroke ends in an identical small radiant point, a four-armed star with the vertical arms slightly shorter than the horizontal ones, about 18% of the canvas width. The strokes are ragged on their left ends, cut flat and clean on the right. The upper stroke runs roughly 62% of the canvas width, the lower roughly 44%, so the eye sees two unequal lines that finish in the same place. That is the radif: the lines differ, the ending does not.

**Palette.** Ground #14161F, an ink blue-black, flat, no gradient. Strokes #E7E2D6, a bone off-white at about 70% opacity so they sit back. The two terminal stars in #E0A33C, a dried-marigold amber, at full opacity. The stars are the only saturated element in the icon.

**At 16px.** The template reduction is two horizontal bars and two aligned dots, four elements on the pixel grid. Drop the star arms and make the terminals square dots at that size. It reads as two short rules with a punctuation mark at the end of each, which is distinct from every play, pause, note and waveform glyph in a menu bar.

**At 512px.** Add a very slight paper grain to the ground. Give the strokes a qalam edge, thicker at the start of the stroke, thinning and fraying at the left cut, so they read as written rather than drawn. Let the amber stars carry a hair of ink bleed at their centres. If you want the star sense from Rekhta, put one additional faint point in the upper-left field at about 8% opacity, low enough that it only appears in Finder at large sizes.

**The risk to design against.** Two stacked horizontal bars can read as a text-alignment or list glyph. Three things defeat it: only two strokes and never four, the strokes deliberately unequal and not left-aligned, and the terminals being the only coloured thing on the canvas. Check it against the SF Symbols alignment icons before committing.

## gamak — the shaken note

**Core shape.** One filled circle, and behind it two progressively fainter copies of the exact same circle, each offset a few degrees along a short arc, as if the note had been struck and had not settled. Primary circle at roughly 26% of canvas width, centred slightly right and below middle. Copy one offset up-left along the arc by about 40% of a radius at 45% opacity. Copy two continues the arc at about 75% of a radius at 20% opacity. The offsets sit on a curve, not a straight line, so it reads as oscillation rather than a misaligned stack or a drop shadow.

**Palette.** Ground #1A1512, a warm near-black in the direction of tanpura wood, flat. Primary circle #F2EDE4, bone. Trailing copy one in #4FA8A0, a muted teal. Trailing copy two in #C08A4A, a copper. Picking a cool and a warm for the two ghosts is deliberate: this app derives its accent colours from cover art, and two different hues behind one white note hints at that without turning into a rainbow. No red.

**At 16px.** Template reduction is three same-size circles at 100%, 45% and 20% alpha along the short arc. Alpha survives in a template image and the tint is applied by the system, so the smear reads. It looks like a dot with a wake, which nothing else in a menu bar is doing.

**At 512px.** Soften the two trailing copies with a small blur so they read as motion rather than as three flat discs. Give the primary circle a faint, very tight inner shadow at its lower-right so it sits forward. Nothing else. This icon should stay almost empty at large size.

**Relation to the name.** Gamak is the movement inside a note. The icon is one note that is visibly moving and nothing else on the canvas. If someone asks, the answer is one sentence.

---

## sneck — the lifted latch

**Core shape.** A forged iron door latch, mid-lift. A thick horizontal bar, about 60% of the canvas width, rotated roughly 12 degrees counter-clockwise. A small round pivot boss at its left end. A square keeper block at the lower right that the bar's tip has just cleared, with visible daylight between the tip and the keeper. That gap is the whole idea: the latch is up, the door is open, you are in.

**Palette.** Ground #1F2A25, a deep matte green-slate, flat, matte, no sheen. This matters: a glassy gradient ground puts it back in the pile of media icons. Latch and keeper in a warm pewter, base #A8ADA6, with a single directional highlight along the bar's top edge in #C9B27A brass. Pivot boss slightly darker than the bar so it reads as a separate piece. No red.

**At 16px.** Three elements is tight but it works if the bar is 2px on the grid and the pivot and keeper are single filled squares. Hand-draw it. The silhouette is a short tilted rule with a dot at one end and a block near the other, and the gap between the bar tip and the keeper needs at least one clear pixel or the whole point is lost.

**At 512px.** This is the one icon that earns material detail. Chamfer the bar's long edges. Add a light hammer texture across the top face, four or five shallow facets, irregular. Put a soft directional light from the upper left so the chamfer catches. Leave the ground completely flat so the iron is the only thing with any depth in the frame.

**Relation to the name.** The mark is literally a sneck. If you take this name, the icon is the definition, which is the strongest name-to-icon coupling of the three.

# PART 3 — THE HONEST CAVEAT

**The single biggest risk with radif: the name does no selling.**

Every other property is good. It cleared everywhere, the domains are free, the Homebrew token is free, nobody commercial holds the string, and I verified the one flagged language risk is not real. What it cannot do is tell a stranger anything. Someone scrolling r/macapps sees "radif" and gets nothing. `shuck` had an English verb underneath it that snapped into place the moment you heard the explanation. `radif` snaps into place only for people who read ghazals, which is a real audience, maybe the right audience, but it is small and it is not the audience that installs Mac apps. You will end up writing the same explanatory sentence in every thread, every README, and every reply for the life of the project.

Worth saying plainly alongside that: your entire naming corpus is English words and abbreviations. shuck, greens, vemb, vibecheck, brandmd, worththeauto, gstudio. Not one Indian-language name in the set. Going to radif is a departure. I think it is the right departure, because this is the one project where the poetry and the music are the same thing and there is no reason to keep signing it in a borrowed language. But it is a departure and you should make it knowing that.

**What would make me switch to gamak.**

One test, ten minutes, do it before you commit. Write both names in the four places they will actually live: the Dock label, `brew install --cask radif` against `brew install --cask gamak`, `github.com/yuvrajangadsingh/radif` against the same with gamak, and a Reddit title that starts "I built X, a native Mac client for". Then say each one out loud to somebody who does not read ghazals and watch whether they ask what it means.

If radif needs a sentence every single time and gamak does not, take gamak. Gamak is the name that argues the product's case to a stranger, the movement inside a note, which is the whole reason you built a player that chases 271k. Radif is the name that signs it. Both are defensible, both are clean where it counts, and the difference is whether you want the name to explain the app or to mark it as yours.

The one thing that would move me off both and onto sneck is if house consistency matters more to you than meaning. Sneck is the only candidate in the top three that makes the exact `shuck` move, and it has the cleanest trademark position of anything on the list. The price is that people will glance it as `sneak` and `snake`, and for an unofficial client of a Google service, `sneak` is the wrong shadow to carry for free.

Keeping the current name is not an option worth arguing. It is YouTube-derived, which is the thing Google's brand rules specifically reject, and it collides with the upstream fork. Those are two independent reasons and neither goes away with time.
